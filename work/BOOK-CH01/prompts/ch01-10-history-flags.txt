TOPIC: Ask the question twice  (book: Cardiothoracic Surgery 2e, chapter: Preoperative assessment, pages 4).
Teach exactly these facts, no others; every label text must come from them:
- Ask the question twice.
- Any other medical problems? Ever in hospital for anything other than the heart? Any operations, ever?
- Be persistent: patients often forget important chronic illnesses and operations.
- Previous chest radiotherapy means a CT scan, because the aorta may be calcified.
- Malignant disease needs careful evaluation: in most cases, metastatic spread is a relative contraindication to cardiac surgery.
- Thyroid disease may dictate the choice of antiarrhythmic.
- A hip replacement means a pillow between the legs when transferring, because adducting the hip can dislocate it.
- Blood dyscrasias, thrombocytopenia, factor deficiencies, thrombophilias, need a hematologist.
- And diabetes has its own pre-op plan.
- Every flag has an action.
