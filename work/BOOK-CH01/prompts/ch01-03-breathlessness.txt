TOPIC: How breathless is the patient?  (book: Cardiothoracic Surgery 2e, chapter: Preoperative assessment, pages 2).
Teach exactly these facts, no others; every label text must come from them:
- How breathless is the patient, and why?
- Start with exertional dyspnea and classify it on the New York Heart Association scale.
- Class one: no limitation during ordinary activity. Class two: slight limitation.
- Class three: marked limitation. Class four: no physical activity without symptoms.
- Heart failure is defined as class three or four.
- The same scale also grades angina.
- Then look for other precipitants: paroxysmal nocturnal dyspnea, orthopnea, palpitations.
- Chest infections, COPD, pulmonary embolism, and drugs such as β-blockers can all contribute.
- Ask about associated symptoms: peripheral edema, palpitations, wheeze, and sputum.
- And what helps: NTG, rest, or sitting up.
- Classify it. Define failure at class three. Then hunt for the cause.
