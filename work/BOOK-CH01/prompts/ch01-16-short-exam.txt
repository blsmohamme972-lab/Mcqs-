TOPIC: The short examination  (book: Cardiothoracic Surgery 2e, chapter: Preoperative assessment, pages 6).
Teach exactly these facts, no others; every label text must come from them:
- Eight steps make the short examination.
- Its purposes: find disease that needs investigating, help plan the surgery, and spot problems that may delay recovery.
- For the average elective patient, a shortened version is sufficient.
- One: pulse, rate and rhythm. Two: blood pressure.
- Three: jugular venous pressure, with the patient at 45°.
- Four: auscultate the heart sounds, the carotids, and the breath sounds, and look for scars.
- Five: the abdomen: tenderness, hernias, organomegaly, scars.
- Six: feel the peripheral pulses, including the abdominal aorta.
- Seven: assess pedal edema. Eight: assess the conduits, the vessels that may become grafts.
- Pulse, pressure, veins, chest, abdomen, pulses, edema, conduits.
