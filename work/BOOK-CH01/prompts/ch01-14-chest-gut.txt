TOPIC: A cold can cancel the operation  (book: Cardiothoracic Surgery 2e, chapter: Preoperative assessment, pages 5).
Teach exactly these facts, no others; every label text must come from them:
- A cold can cancel the operation.
- An active upper respiratory tract infection is sufficient reason to cancel, so ask about cough, colds and sputum.
- If you suspect one, measure the temperature, C-reactive protein and white cell count, and get a chest X-ray.
- Assess COPD by asking about admissions, inhalers, nebulizers and home oxygen.
- If breathlessness dominates, get lung function tests, including blood gases.
- Now the gut. Cardiac surgery carries a risk of upper G I bleeding from peptic ulcers, prolonged ileus, and flares of pancreatitis or cholecystitis.
- So ask about dyspepsia, ulcers and pancreatitis.
- Start every patient on a proton pump inhibitor.
- Flag the drug chart: no NSAIDs in at-risk patients, and give aspirin enteric-coated, with food.
- Lungs: infection and COPD. Gut: ask, protect, avoid NSAIDs.
