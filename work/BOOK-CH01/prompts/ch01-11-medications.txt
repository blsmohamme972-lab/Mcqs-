TOPIC: Every drug has a clock  (book: Cardiothoracic Surgery 2e, chapter: Preoperative assessment, pages 4).
Teach exactly these facts, no others; every label text must come from them:
- Every drug has a clock.
- Antianginals and β-blockers continue until surgery.
- Afterwards, antianginals stop; β-blockers return as pressure and rhythm allow.
- ACE inhibitors stop 24–48 hours before, to avoid profound peripheral dilatation afterwards.
- Aspirin and clopidogrel: many surgeons stop them 5–10 days before, because they increase bleeding.
- But ischemic risk rises slightly, so high-risk patients, such as recent PCI or a tight left main, may continue.
- Warfarin stops five days before. If thromboembolic risk is high, admit early for intravenous heparin.
- Steroids: above five milligrams of prednisone daily, give hydrocortisone cover from the premedication, to avoid an Addisonian crisis.
- Long-acting hypoglycemics are withheld on the morning of surgery, with an insulin sliding scale if needed.
- Continue, stop, or decide: know the clock for each.
