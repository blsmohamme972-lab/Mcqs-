TOPIC: Pulse, pressure and face  (book: Cardiothoracic Surgery 2e, chapter: Preoperative assessment, pages 6).
Teach exactly these facts, no others; every label text must come from them:
- Pulse, pressure and face each point to a diagnosis.
- Rate and rhythm at the radial pulse. Character and volume at the brachial or carotid.
- Aortic stenosis classically gives a slow-rising, thready pulse.
- Aortic incompetence gives a large-volume bounding pulse.
- Blood pressure: a systolic above 180 should be treated with nifedipine, ten milligrams sublingually, before surgery.
- A wide pulse pressure suggests aortic incompetence.
- Use a broad cuff for large arms, or the pressure is underestimated.
- Check both arms if you suspect aortic dissection or coarctation.
- The face: a malar flush, cyanosis in a butterfly distribution, is characteristic of mitral stenosis.
- In the eyes: jaundice from hemolysis with prosthetic valves, xanthelasma and corneal arcus with hyperlipidemia, and Roth spots in endocarditis.
- Slow pulse, wide pressure, malar flush: three signs, three valves.
