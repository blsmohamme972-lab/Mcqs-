TOPIC: A previous operation changes the next one  (book: Cardiothoracic Surgery 2e, chapter: Preoperative assessment, pages 2).
Teach exactly these facts, no others; every label text must come from them:
- A previous operation changes the next one.
- Find it three ways: ask, check the chart, and look.
- Look carefully at the whole chest, and the back, for scars.
- A scar is the quickest clue when the history is vague.
- Previous cardiac surgery adds to operative mortality, and needs several changes to the standard approach.
- That added risk is incremental, on top of every other factor.
- That is why most patients should have a CT of the chest.
- It shows how the aorta and the right ventricle sit behind the sternum.
- And where the patent bypass grafts run.
- Those relationships decide how safely the chest can be reopened.
- The scan needs intravenous contrast.
- And always obtain the operative report: it records what was done.
- Scars. CT. Contrast. Report.
