TOPIC: Social history is part of the surgical plan  (book: Cardiothoracic Surgery 2e, chapter: Preoperative assessment, pages 5).
Teach exactly these facts, no others; every label text must come from them:
- Social history is part of the surgical plan.
- Jehovah's Witness patients do not accept blood or blood products, and some refuse cell salvage.
- Identify them early, counsel them, document their preferences, and aim for a hematocrit above forty percent.
- Several weeks of erythropoietin may be indicated.
- Heavy or binge drinkers risk alcohol withdrawal.
- Avoid haloperidol: it lowers the seizure threshold and prolongs the QT interval.
- Prescribe diazepam, two milligrams orally every eight hours, with thiamine and folate.
- If the patient lives alone, plan rehabilitation.
- If they use a stick or frame, involve physiotherapists early, because sternal union may be compromised even if mobility is not.
- And ask about TIAs, strokes and residual deficit, and identify dementia: advanced dementia is a contraindication to cardiac surgery.
- Blood, alcohol, home, brain.
