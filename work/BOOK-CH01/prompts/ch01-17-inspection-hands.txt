TOPIC: Examine from the end of the bed  (book: Cardiothoracic Surgery 2e, chapter: Preoperative assessment, pages 6).
Teach exactly these facts, no others; every label text must come from them:
- Examine from the end of the bed.
- Is the patient comfortable at rest, or requiring oxygen? Well, or moribund? Obese, or cachectic?
- Look for features of Down's syndrome, which suggests an atrial or ventricular septal defect, and Marfan syndrome, which suggests aortic aneurysm, dissection and incompetence.
- Then position the patient at 45°.
- Now the hands and forearms. Nicotine staining.
- And the stigmata of endocarditis: splinter hemorrhages.
- Osler's nodes: tender lumps in the fingertip pulps.
- Janeway lesions: red macules on the palms or the flexor aspects of the wrists.
- Tendon xanthomas. And petechiae, which may be present in infective endocarditis.
- Bed-end, syndromes, hands: what the surface says about valves and aorta.
