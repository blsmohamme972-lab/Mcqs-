TOPIC: Preoperative assessment: the map  (book: Cardiothoracic Surgery 2e, chapter: Preoperative assessment, pages 1-6).
Teach exactly these facts, no others; every label text must come from them:
- Before the first incision, thirteen topics decide whether and how we operate.
- Three happen at the bedside: the cardiovascular history, the systems enquiry, and the examination.
- Nine are investigations: routine laboratory tests, the chest radiograph, computed tomography, cardiac catheterization, coronary imaging, hemodynamics, echocardiography, echo interpretation, and perfusion and viability.
- The last is the general pre-op checklist.
- The cardiovascular history has three aims: establish the indications for surgery, identify issues that need management outside standard protocols, and define the preoperative estimate of risk.
- The systems enquiry looks for problems that alter standard management, contraindications to elective surgery, and problems that could delay discharge.
- The examination finds disease that needs investigating, helps plan the surgery, and spots problems that may delay recovery.
- So ask every time: why operate, what must change, and what could go wrong.
