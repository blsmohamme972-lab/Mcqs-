TOPIC: A black-out is not always the heart  (book: Cardiothoracic Surgery 2e, chapter: Preoperative assessment, pages 3).
Teach exactly these facts, no others; every label text must come from them:
- A black-out is not always the heart.
- Think epilepsy, TIAs, low blood sugar, or a vasovagal faint.
- Vasovagal faints follow standing, emotion, coughing, or passing urine.
- True cardiac syncope from aortic stenosis is usually exertional.
- If it happens sitting or lying, think arrhythmia.
- Dizziness is different: it can come before a faint, but it is fairly non-specific.
- Common culprits are drugs: antianginals, antihypertensives, and diuretics, causing postural hypotension.
- It can also reflect low cardiac output, from failure, arrhythmia or ischemia, or cerebrovascular disease.
- And do not forget diabetic autonomic neuropathy: probably under-recognized.
- When did it happen? What were they doing? What are they taking?
