TOPIC: Angina is not always chest pain  (book: Cardiothoracic Surgery 2e, chapter: Preoperative assessment, pages 2).
Teach exactly these facts, no others; every label text must come from them:
- Angina is not always a crushing chest pain.
- The textbook picture is central, crushing pain, spreading to the jaw and down the left arm.
- But patients may describe heaviness, indigestion, pain only in the arm, neck or jaw, nausea, or just fatigue.
- So ask what brings it on: exertion, stress, emotion, and cold air.
- And what relieves it: rest, or sublingual nitroglycerine.
- Then count the spray: how many times a day do they need it?
- Finally, grade it. The Canadian Cardiovascular Society scale applies to angina only.
- Class one: no angina with ordinary activity. Class two: slight limitation.
- Class three: marked limitation. Class four: angina on minimal exertion, or at rest.
- Classes three and four carry increased risk.
- Describe it, trigger it, relieve it, count it, grade it.
